# After the launch

Launch traffic fades in days. What lasts is what you capture and the
relationships you start.

## The first week

- **Follow up** with everyone who commented, asked or signed up and went quiet: a
  personal thank-you, an answer, a question about their use. Each send approved by
  the person.
- **Onboarding**: new users get a short welcome sequence that leads them to the
  first useful result (not a feature tour).
- **Fix** the top three problems people reported, and tell those who reported
  them.
- **Results post**: a short, honest write-up - numbers, what worked, what did not,
  what is next. People share lessons more than announcements.

## Measure

Compare channels by what matters for the person's goal (from the product brief),
not by vanity numbers:

| Measure | From |
|---|---|
| Visits and sign-ups per channel | analytics, links with UTM tags |
| Activated users (reached the first useful result) | the product |
| Paying customers or qualified leads | billing or CRM |
| Feedback themes | comments, emails, support |

Write what to repeat and what to drop next time.

## Keep going

- Every meaningful release is a new launch moment (launch-plan: big, medium,
  small).
- Space announcements out; keep a public changelog.
- Grow owned channels: invite visitors to the list or community at every step.
- Comparison pages, guides and examples keep bringing people after the spike.
