# Directories, newsletters, communities, press

## Launch directories

Sites that list new products. Each brings a little traffic and a backlink; few
bring many users. Use a handful that fit the product type rather than dozens:

- general startup and maker lists (for example BetaList for pre-launch products,
  Indie Hackers' product pages);
- for developer tools, developer-focused launch sites (for example DevHunt);
- for AI tools, AI tool directories.

Check each one's current rules, review queue and any fees before submitting;
never pay for "guaranteed upvotes" or similar.

## Newsletters

A mention in a newsletter its readers trust can beat a day on a big platform.

- Find newsletters the audience reads (ask a few users; search the niche).
- Many have a "submit a tool" form or a curator who takes tips. Send one short,
  personal note: what it is, why their readers would care, the link.
- Sponsoring is an option when budget allows; measure it like any ad.

## Communities

Slack and Discord groups, forums, membership communities, local meetups.

- Be a member who helps before you announce anything.
- Follow each community's rules for promotion (often a dedicated channel).
- A personal story and a real question work better than an announcement.

## Press

Press is a tool, not a goal: decide what it is for first (credibility for
sales, hiring, investors, or a burst of awareness). For most first launches,
creators, newsletters and communities matter more.

- Pitch a specific journalist whose recent articles match the story - never a
  mass blast.
- The story must be worth telling for their readers, not for the company: what
  is new, surprising or useful.
- Keep a simple press kit ready (see launch-assets).

(Press points draw on insights collected in `RefoundAI/lenny-skills`, MIT.)
