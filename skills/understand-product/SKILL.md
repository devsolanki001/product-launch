# Understand the product

Good launch work starts from what the product really is, in its own users'
words - not from what the founder hopes it is, and never from guesses.

## Read first, ask second

Read everything the person gave or that is public:

- the website or landing page (headline, pricing, sign-up)
- the repository: README, docs, examples, recent changes, license, stars and
  issues (what people ask for, what breaks)
- the app store page and its reviews
- a demo video, screenshots, the changelog
- try it, when you can: sign up or install, and note what happened in the first
  five minutes

Then ask the person only what you could not find. Typical gaps: who uses it
today, what they used before, what it costs, what is already planned for launch.

## Product type

Name one. It decides the channels and the assets.

| Type | Examples | Where its early users usually are |
|---|---|---|
| Developer tool / API / CLI | a library, an SDK, a dev platform | GitHub, Hacker News, dev communities, X, newsletters for developers |
| Open-source project | a framework, a self-hostable app | GitHub, Hacker News, Reddit's relevant subs, awesome-lists |
| AI tool or agent | an assistant, an agent skill, a model app | X, Product Hunt, AI newsletters, Reddit, YouTube demos |
| B2B SaaS | a tool for a team or job role | LinkedIn, the job's communities, newsletters, direct outreach, Product Hunt |
| Consumer web app | a tool for personal use | Product Hunt, TikTok/Instagram/YouTube short video, Reddit, X |
| Mobile app | iOS/Android | app stores (store page and keywords), short video, Reddit, Product Hunt |
| Digital product / content | a course, template, ebook, newsletter | the creator's audience, X, LinkedIn, Gumroad-like marketplaces, communities |
| Marketplace / community | two-sided or member-based | the supply side first, niche communities, direct outreach |

A product can be two (an open-source dev tool with a hosted SaaS); pick the
primary one for the launch.

## The product brief (one page)

Write it in plain words, with facts only from what you read or the person said;
mark gaps as `[unknown: ...]`.

1. **What it is** - one sentence a non-expert understands.
2. **Product type** - from the table.
3. **Who it is for** - the specific person and the situation they are in.
4. **What they do today instead** - the real alternative, often a spreadsheet, a
   manual process, a bigger tool, or nothing.
5. **What it does better** - the one or two things, with proof if any.
6. **How to try it** - the path from link to first result, and how long it takes.
7. **Price** - and whether there is a free way in.
8. **Proof** - real users, numbers, quotes, stars, reviews (only real ones).
9. **State** - beta, launched, version; what is missing.
10. **What the person wants from the launch** - users, paying customers,
    feedback, investors, hires. Ask if unclear; it changes the plan.

Show the brief to the person and fix what is wrong before going on - every later
stage builds on it.
