# Positioning

Positioning is the context that makes a product's value obvious to the right
person. Without it, every post and page reads like "an AI-powered platform for
everything" and nobody remembers it. Do this before any copy.

## Work through five questions

Use the product brief (understand-product). For each, write the answer from facts
and the person's words - ask when you do not know.

1. **The real alternative** - if this product disappeared, what would its users
   do instead? Usually not a famous competitor but a spreadsheet, a manual
   process, a general tool, or doing nothing. That is what you are up against.
2. **What only this does** - the features or approach the alternative lacks.
3. **The value of that** - what those features mean for the user, in outcomes:
   time, money, risk, a thing they could not do before. One or two, measurable if
   possible.
4. **Who cares most** - the people for whom that value matters most, specific
   enough to find them ("solo founders who just shipped their first app", not
   "businesses").
5. **The frame** - the category or comparison that makes the value obvious
   instantly ("Like X, but for Y"; "the open-source alternative to X"; a new
   category only if there is truly none - new categories are expensive to teach).

(This order follows April Dunford's positioning method - alternatives first,
then differentiation, value, customers and frame; see her book "Obviously
Awesome". The questions are written for this skill.)

## What changed in the world

Strong launches name a shift: something new makes the old way look outdated
(a new technology, a new rule, a new habit). "Teams now ship with AI agents, but
nobody checks what the agents did" is a stronger opening than "We built an agent
checker." Name the shift only if it is real.

## Write it down

Produce, in this order:

- **One-liner** (under 12 words): what it is and for whom.
- **Tagline** (under 60 characters, also fits Product Hunt): the value.
- **Three proof points**: each a claim and its evidence (only real evidence).
- **The foil**: the old way you replace, said without attacking competitors by
  name unless the person wants comparison pages.
- **Words to use and avoid**: the user's own words from reviews, issues and
  conversations; avoid buzzwords ("revolutionary", "seamless", "AI-powered" as a
  claim on its own).

## Mistakes to catch

- **"Better" instead of "different"** - slightly better features rarely move
  anyone from what they already use.
- **Ignoring the status quo** - the biggest competitor is usually the habit.
- **Too broad** - "for everyone" means for no one; launch for the group who will
  care most, then widen.
- **Jargon** - if a non-technical friend cannot repeat it, rewrite it.
