# Outreach

Outreach is the person speaking to people one at a time. Done well it is the
highest-return part of a launch; done badly it is spam that follows the founder
for years.

## Rules

- **Every message is approved by the person before it is sent** - the exact text
  and the exact recipient. In Vonit, each send is an action the person approves.
- **Personal only**: every message is written for its recipient, referring to
  something real about them (their work, their audience, why this fits). No
  templates with only the name swapped.
- **Caps**: at most 20 new people a day, and only people with a reason to care.
  Never bought or scraped lists; never someone who asked not to be contacted.
- **Real addresses only**: from the person, a public page where the recipient
  invites contact (a "pitch me" page, a contact form, a public email for tips), or
  a mutual introduction - never guessed addresses.
- **Easy no**: one follow-up at most, a week later; then stop.
- **Honest**: say who you are and that you made the product; follow the email and
  privacy laws where the recipient lives (for example an easy way to opt out of
  any repeated email).

## Build the list (in rings, closest first)

1. **People who already care**: users, beta testers, the waitlist, friends,
   colleagues, people who gave feedback. Tell them first and personally.
2. **People whose audience fits**: creators, newsletter curators, community
   organizers, podcast hosts in the niche. Find them where the audience is (the
   channels chosen in launch-channels). For each, note why they fit.
3. **Press**, only if the press goal is clear (see the press notes in
   launch-channels).

For each person: name, where to reach them (from an allowed source), why this
fits them, what you are asking (try it, a mention, feedback), status.

## Write the message

- Subject or first line: specific to them.
- Two or three sentences: who you are, what you made, why it fits their audience
  or work.
- One clear, small ask - try it, or "would this be useful for your readers?".
  Never ask for upvotes.
- The link, and something useful they can use even if they say no (early access,
  a free account for their readers, data).

## Track

Keep the list with statuses (drafted, approved, sent, replied, done) so the
launch can be followed and nobody is contacted twice.
